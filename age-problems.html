Age Problems

RRB Group D Mathematics • Chapter 19

About this chapter

Age problems translate statements about present, past and future ages into equations.

Study tip: Read the rules, practise the worked examples, then attempt the quiz without looking at the notes.

1. Key concepts and rules

  • Let a person's present age be x years.
  • After n years, age = x+n.
  • n years ago, age = x−n.
  • If a ratio of ages is given, represent ages using a common multiplier.
  • The difference between two people's ages remains constant.

2. Important formulas and reminders

Age after n years = Present age + n
Age n years ago = Present age − n
If present ages are in ratio a:b, write them as ax and bx
Age difference = Older age − Younger age

3. Solved examples

Example 1: A person is 24 years old. How old will the person be after 8 years?

24+8.

Answer: 32 years

Example 2: A father is 40 and his son is 10. What is the age difference?

40−10.

Answer: 30 years

Example 3: Present ages are in ratio 3:2 and their sum is 40. Find the ages.

5 parts = 40, so one part = 8.

Answer: 24 years and 16 years

4. Exam tips

  • Write down the given values before selecting a formula.
  • Check that units are consistent.
  • Do calculations step by step and simplify the final answer.
  • Estimate the result to catch arithmetic mistakes.
  • Practise questions under a time limit after learning the method.
