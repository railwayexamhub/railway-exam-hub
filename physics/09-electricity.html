Electricity

RRB Group D General Science · Physics · Chapter 09

About this chapter

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge through a cross-section of a conductor.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Current I = Q/t and is measured in ampere (A). Conventional current direction is the direction of positive charge flow.
  2. Potential difference is work done per unit charge and is measured in volt (V). Resistance opposes current and is measured in ohm (Ω).
  3. Ohm's law states that current through a conductor is directly proportional to potential difference when physical conditions such as temperature remain constant.
  4. In a series circuit, current is the same through all components and resistances add. In parallel, potential difference is common and reciprocal resistances add.
  5. Electrical power is the rate of electrical energy transfer. For a resistor, P = VI = I²R = V²/R.
  6. A fuse is connected in series and melts when excessive current flows, protecting wiring. Earthing provides a low-resistance safety path.
  7. Good conductors have many mobile charge carriers; insulators have very few. Copper and aluminium are common conductors.
  8. Domestic electricity is dangerous; never touch exposed live wires. Safety devices and qualified electricians are essential.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • I = Q/t
  • V = IR
  • P = VI = I²R = V²/R
  • E = Pt

3. Worked examples

A 12 V supply is connected to a 4 Ω resistor. Find current.

Answer: 3 A

I = V/R = 12/4 = 3 A.
