Sound

RRB Group D General Science · Physics · Chapter 07

About this chapter

Sound is a mechanical wave produced by vibrating sources and transmitted through a material medium.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Sound needs a medium; it cannot travel through vacuum. In air, sound is commonly transmitted as longitudinal pressure waves.
  2. A longitudinal wave has compressions and rarefactions. Particles oscillate parallel to the direction of wave propagation.
  3. Frequency is the number of oscillations per second and is measured in hertz (Hz). It determines pitch.
  4. Amplitude is related to the energy carried by a wave and affects loudness. Loudness also depends on the ear and distance.
  5. The speed of sound depends on the medium and its conditions. It is generally faster in solids than in gases.
  6. The human audible range is approximately 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. Below this is infrasound; above it is ultrasound.
  7. Echo is reflected sound heard separately when the time gap is sufficient. Reflection is used in SONAR and medical ultrasound imaging.
  8. Noise pollution can affect sleep, concentration and hearing. Ear protection and reducing exposure help prevent damage.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • wave speed v = fλ
  • time period T = 1/f
  • echo distance d = vt/2

3. Worked examples

A wave has frequency 200 Hz and wavelength 1.5 m. Find speed.

Answer: 300 m/s

v = fλ = 200 × 1.5 = 300 m/s.
