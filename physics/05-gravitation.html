Gravitation

RRB Group D General Science · Physics · Chapter 05

About this chapter

Gravitation is the universal attractive force between any two masses.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Newton's law of universal gravitation: every two masses attract with force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of their separation.
  2. The gravitational force acts along the line joining the centres of the two bodies and is always attractive for ordinary masses.
  3. Near Earth's surface, acceleration due to gravity is approximately 9.8 m/s²; it is commonly rounded to 10 m/s² in simple calculations.
  4. Mass is the amount of matter and remains essentially constant with location. Weight is gravitational force and changes with local gravitational acceleration.
  5. Weight W = mg. SI unit of weight is newton; SI unit of mass is kilogram.
  6. An object in free fall experiences gravity as the only significant force if air resistance is neglected.
  7. Pressure is force per unit area. Liquid pressure increases with depth because of the weight of liquid above.
  8. Buoyant force is the upward force exerted by a fluid. An object floats when buoyancy can balance its weight.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • F = Gm₁m₂/r²
  • weight W = mg
  • pressure P = F/A
  • liquid pressure p = ρgh

3. Worked examples

Find the weight of a 6 kg object where g = 10 m/s².

Answer: 60 N

W = mg = 6 × 10 = 60 N.
