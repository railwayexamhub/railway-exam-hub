Work, Energy and Power

RRB Group D General Science · Physics · Chapter 04

About this chapter

Work is done when a force causes displacement in its direction or has a component along the displacement.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. For constant force, work W = Fs cos θ. Work is positive when force helps displacement, negative when it opposes it, and zero when perpendicular or when there is no displacement.
  2. The SI unit of work and energy is joule (J). One joule is the work done by a force of 1 N through 1 m in its direction.
  3. Kinetic energy is energy due to motion: KE = ½mv². It increases with mass and with the square of speed.
  4. Gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface is PE = mgh, measured relative to a chosen reference level.
  5. The law of conservation of energy states that energy is not created or destroyed; it changes form. Total energy of an isolated system remains constant.
  6. Power is the rate of doing work or transferring energy. Its SI unit is watt (W), where 1 W = 1 J/s.
  7. Commercial electrical energy is often measured in kilowatt-hour. 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.
  8. Machines may make work more convenient by changing force or direction; in ideal machines, input work equals output work.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • W = Fs cos θ
  • KE = ½mv²
  • PE = mgh
  • P = W/t
  • 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J

3. Worked examples

A 10 N force moves an object 3 m in the force direction. Find work.

Answer: 30 J

W = Fs = 10 × 3 = 30 J.
