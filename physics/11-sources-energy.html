Sources of Energy

RRB Group D General Science · Physics · Chapter 11

About this chapter

Energy sources are resources from which useful energy is obtained for domestic, industrial and transport needs.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Renewable sources are replenished naturally on a human timescale, such as solar, wind, hydro and sustainably managed biomass.
  2. Non-renewable sources such as coal, petroleum, natural gas and uranium are finite or form too slowly for rapid replacement.
  3. Fossil fuels release energy by combustion but emit carbon dioxide and pollutants; efficient use and pollution controls are important.
  4. Solar photovoltaic cells convert sunlight directly into electricity; solar thermal systems use solar heat.
  5. Wind turbines convert kinetic energy of moving air into electrical energy. Output depends on wind speed and site conditions.
  6. Hydroelectricity uses falling or flowing water to turn turbines. Dams can affect ecosystems and communities.
  7. Biogas is produced by anaerobic decomposition of organic matter and contains mainly methane and carbon dioxide.
  8. Energy conservation reduces demand through efficient appliances, insulation, public transport and avoiding unnecessary consumption.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • efficiency = useful output energy/input energy × 100%
  • 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J

3. Worked examples

A device receives 500 J and gives 350 J useful output. Find efficiency.

Answer: 70%

Efficiency = 350/500 × 100 = 70%.
