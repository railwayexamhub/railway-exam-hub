Magnetism and Electromagnetism

RRB Group D General Science · Physics · Chapter 10

About this chapter

Magnetism is associated with magnetic fields produced by magnets and moving electric charges.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. A magnet has north and south poles. Like poles repel and unlike poles attract; magnetic poles occur in pairs in ordinary magnets.
  2. Magnetic field is the region where a magnetic force can be experienced. Field lines outside a bar magnet run from north to south.
  3. A compass needle aligns approximately with Earth's local magnetic field and is used to find direction.
  4. Electric current produces a magnetic field around a conductor. The right-hand thumb rule gives the field direction around a straight wire.
  5. A solenoid produces a field similar to a bar magnet; inserting a soft iron core makes an electromagnet stronger.
  6. Electromagnets are used in electric bells, relays, cranes and motors because their magnetism can be switched or varied.
  7. An electric motor converts electrical energy into mechanical energy. A generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy.
  8. Electromagnetic induction is production of an emf when magnetic flux linked with a circuit changes.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • F = BIL sinθ
  • Faraday induction: induced emf ∝ rate of change of magnetic flux
  • motor: electrical → mechanical
  • generator: mechanical → electrical

3. Worked examples

A 2 m wire carries 3 A perpendicular to a 0.5 T field. Find force.

Answer: 3 N

F = BIL = 0.5 × 3 × 2 = 3 N.
