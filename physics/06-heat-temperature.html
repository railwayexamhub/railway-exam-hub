Heat and Temperature

RRB Group D General Science · Physics · Chapter 06

About this chapter

Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference; temperature indicates how hot or cold a body is.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Temperature is measured in kelvin (SI), degree Celsius and degree Fahrenheit. Heat is energy and is measured in joules.
  2. Heat flows spontaneously from a higher-temperature body to a lower-temperature body until thermal equilibrium is reached.
  3. Conduction transfers heat through direct interactions, especially in solids. Metals are generally good thermal conductors.
  4. Convection transfers heat by bulk movement of fluids; it occurs in liquids and gases, not in rigid solids as a bulk flow.
  5. Radiation transfers energy by electromagnetic waves and does not require a material medium; sunlight reaches Earth this way.
  6. Specific heat capacity is the heat needed to raise the temperature of unit mass of a substance by one kelvin.
  7. During a change of state, latent heat is absorbed or released while temperature remains constant for a pure substance at fixed pressure.
  8. Thermal expansion is the increase in dimensions of a material when heated; gaps are left in railway tracks and bridges to accommodate expansion.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • Q = mcΔT
  • Q = mL
  • K = °C + 273.15
  • °F = (9/5)°C + 32

3. Worked examples

How much heat raises 2 kg of a substance with c = 500 J/kg°C by 4°C?

Answer: 4000 J

Q = mcΔT = 2 × 500 × 4 = 4000 J.
