Force and Laws of Motion

RRB Group D General Science · Physics · Chapter 03

About this chapter

Force is an interaction that can change an object's state of rest, uniform motion, direction or shape.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Newton's first law: an object remains at rest or in uniform straight-line motion unless acted on by a net external force. This property is inertia.
  2. Inertia depends on mass. Greater mass means greater resistance to a change in motion.
  3. Newton's second law states that net force equals rate of change of momentum. For constant mass, F = ma.
  4. Momentum is the product of mass and velocity (p = mv). Its SI unit is kg m/s.
  5. Newton's third law: every action has an equal and opposite reaction. The forces act on different bodies, so they do not cancel each other on one body.
  6. Impulse equals change in momentum and is also force multiplied by the time interval when force is constant.
  7. Friction opposes relative motion or its tendency. It can provide grip for walking and braking, but also causes wear and heating.
  8. Seat belts and airbags increase the time over which momentum changes, reducing average impact force.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • F = ma
  • momentum p = mv
  • impulse = FΔt = Δp
  • weight W = mg

3. Worked examples

A 5 kg object accelerates at 2 m/s². Find the net force.

Answer: 10 N

F = ma = 5 × 2 = 10 N.
