Motion

RRB Group D General Science · Physics · Chapter 02

About this chapter

Motion describes change in position with time relative to a chosen reference point.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Distance is the total length of the actual path and is a scalar; displacement is the shortest directed change from initial to final position and is a vector.
  2. Speed is distance per unit time. Velocity is displacement per unit time and includes direction. Average speed uses total distance divided by total time.
  3. Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. It may be positive, negative (retardation in a chosen direction), or zero.
  4. A distance–time graph has slope equal to speed. A velocity–time graph has slope equal to acceleration and signed area equal to displacement.
  5. Uniform motion covers equal distances in equal time intervals. Non-uniform motion has changing speed or direction.
  6. SI units: distance and displacement metre (m), time second (s), speed and velocity m/s, acceleration m/s².
  7. For constant acceleration: v = u + at; s = ut + ½at²; v² = u² + 2as. These equations require straight-line motion with constant acceleration.
  8. Convert km/h to m/s by multiplying by 5/18; convert m/s to km/h by multiplying by 18/5.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • speed = distance/time
  • average speed = total distance/total time
  • acceleration = (v-u)/t
  • v = u + at
  • s = ut + ½at²
  • v² = u² + 2as

3. Worked examples

A vehicle travels 150 m in 30 s. Find its average speed.

Answer: 5 m/s

150 ÷ 30 = 5 m/s.

A body changes velocity from 4 m/s to 16 m/s in 3 s. Find acceleration.

Answer: 4 m/s²

(16 − 4) ÷ 3 = 4 m/s².
