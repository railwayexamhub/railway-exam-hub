Fractions and Decimals

RRB Group D Mathematics • Chapter 4

About this chapter

Learn how to compare, simplify and calculate with fractions and decimals. These skills are useful in arithmetic questions involving quantities, money, measurements, percentages and ratios.

Study order: Understand fractions → practise operations → convert fractions and decimals → solve mixed questions.

1. Key concepts

  • Numerator: the number above the fraction bar.
  • Denominator: the number below the fraction bar; it cannot be zero.
  • Proper fraction: numerator is smaller than denominator, for example 3/5.
  • Improper fraction: numerator is equal to or greater than denominator, for example 7/4.
  • Mixed number: a whole number and a proper fraction, for example 1 3/4.
  • Equivalent fractions: fractions with the same value, such as 1/2 and 2/4.
  • Like fractions: fractions with the same denominator.
  • Terminating decimal: a decimal that ends, such as 0.25.

2. Important rules and formulas

Equivalent fraction: a/b = (a × n)/(b × n), where n ≠ 0
Addition: a/b + c/d = (ad + bc)/bd
Subtraction: a/b − c/d = (ad − bc)/bd
Multiplication: a/b × c/d = ac/bd
Division: a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c, where c ≠ 0
Fraction to percentage = fraction × 100%
Decimal to percentage = decimal × 100%
Percentage to decimal = percentage ÷ 100
Remember: For addition or subtraction, first make the denominators equal. For multiplication, multiply numerators and denominators. For division, invert the second fraction and multiply.

3. Fraction operations

Addition with unlike denominators

Find the LCM of the denominators, convert both fractions to equivalent fractions, then add the numerators.

Simplification

Divide the numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor (HCF) until the fraction is in its lowest terms.

Mixed numbers

Convert a mixed number to an improper fraction before multiplying or dividing. For example, 2 1/3 = (2 × 3 + 1)/3 = 7/3.

4. Decimal operations and conversions

  • To add or subtract decimals, align the decimal points and then calculate.
  • To multiply decimals, multiply as whole numbers first, then place the decimal point using the total number of decimal places in the factors.
  • To divide by a decimal, move the decimal point in both numbers the same number of places until the divisor is a whole number.
  • To convert a terminating decimal to a fraction, write it over 10, 100, 1000, etc., and simplify.
0.6 = 6/10 = 3/5
3/8 = 0.375
0.45 = 45/100 = 9/20

5. Solved examples

Example 1: Add 2/3 and 1/4

LCM of 3 and 4 is 12. Therefore, 2/3 = 8/12 and 1/4 = 3/12.

Answer: 8/12 + 3/12 = 11/12.

Example 2: Subtract 5/6 − 1/3

Convert 1/3 to 2/6. Then 5/6 − 2/6 = 3/6.

Answer: 1/2.

Example 3: Multiply 3/5 × 10/9

Multiply: (3 × 10)/(5 × 9) = 30/45. Simplify by 15.

Answer: 2/3.

Example 4: Divide 4/7 by 2/3

Invert the second fraction: 4/7 × 3/2 = 12/14.

Answer: 6/7.

Example 5: Convert 2 3/5 to an improper fraction

(2 × 5 + 3)/5 = 13/5.

Answer: 13/5.

Example 6: Convert 0.375 to a fraction

0.375 = 375/1000. Divide numerator and denominator by 125.

Answer: 3/8.

Example 7: Convert 7/20 to a decimal

7 ÷ 20 = 0.35.

Answer: 0.35.

Example 8: Find 0.4 + 1.25

Write 0.4 as 0.40, then add 0.40 + 1.25.

Answer: 1.65.

Example 9: Find 2.4 × 0.5

24 × 5 = 120. There are two decimal places in total.

Answer: 1.20 = 1.2.

Example 10: Find 3/4 of 80

(3/4) × 80 = 3 × 20.

Answer: 60.

6. Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Never add denominators directly when adding fractions.
  • Always simplify the final fraction if possible.
  • In division, invert only the second fraction.
  • When comparing positive fractions, cross-multiplication can help: compare a × d with c × b for a/b and c/d.
  • Keep decimal points aligned in addition and subtraction.
  • Estimate the answer to check whether the result is reasonable.
