LCM and HCF

RRB Group D Mathematics · Chapter 03

About this chapter

Learn factors, multiples, prime factorisation, HCF (Highest Common Factor), LCM (Least Common Multiple), the division method and common exam-style word problems. Study the methods first, then attempt the 25-question practice test.

FoundationSolved examples25 MCQs

1. Key concepts

  • Factor: A whole number that divides another number exactly. For example, 1, 2, 3 and 6 are factors of 6.
  • Multiple: A number obtained by multiplying a given number by a whole number. Multiples of 4 include 4, 8, 12 and 16.
  • Prime number: A whole number greater than 1 with exactly two factors: 1 and itself.
  • Prime factorisation: Expressing a number as a product of prime numbers. Example: 60 = 2² × 3 × 5.
  • HCF: The greatest positive integer that divides each of the given numbers exactly.
  • LCM: The smallest positive integer that is a multiple of every number in the given set.
  • Co-prime numbers: Two integers whose HCF is 1. They do not need to be prime themselves; for example, 8 and 15.

2. Methods to find HCF and LCM

Method A: Prime factorisation

Write each number as a product of primes.

  • For HCF, multiply only the prime factors common to all numbers, using the smallest power of each.
  • For LCM, include every prime factor that appears, using the greatest power of each.
Example: 36 = 2² × 3² and 48 = 2⁴ × 3
HCF = 2² × 3 = 12
LCM = 2⁴ × 3² = 144

Method B: Division method for HCF

Divide the larger number by the smaller number. Then divide the previous divisor by the remainder. Continue until the remainder is zero. The last non-zero remainder (or final divisor) is the HCF.

Example: HCF of 84 and 30
84 = 30 × 2 + 24
30 = 24 × 1 + 6
24 = 6 × 4 + 0
HCF = 6

Method C: Listing multiples (useful for small numbers)

List the multiples of each number. The first common positive multiple is the LCM.

Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, ...
Multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24, ...
LCM = 24

3. Important formulas and reminders

For two positive integers only:
HCF × LCM = Product of the two numbers
Therefore:
LCM = (First number × Second number) ÷ HCF
For two co-prime positive integers:
HCF = 1 and LCM = their product
Fractions in lowest terms:
HCF of fractions = HCF of numerators ÷ LCM of denominators
LCM of fractions = LCM of numerators ÷ HCF of denominators
Use this rule for positive fractions after expressing them in their lowest terms.
Word-problem clue:
“Together again”, “at the same time” or repeating intervals usually indicates LCM. “Largest possible equal size/group” usually indicates HCF.

Remember: The product relation HCF × LCM = product applies directly to two positive integers. Do not apply it as a general formula to three or more numbers.

4. Solved examples

Example 1 — Find HCF by prime factorisation

Find the HCF of 24 and 36.

24 = 2³ × 3
36 = 2² × 3²
Common factors with smallest powers = 2² × 3 = 12.
Answer: 12

Example 2 — Find LCM by prime factorisation

Find the LCM of 12 and 18.

12 = 2² × 3; 18 = 2 × 3².
Take the greatest powers: 2² × 3² = 4 × 9.
Answer: 36

Example 3 — HCF by division method

Find the HCF of 96 and 36.

96 = 36 × 2 + 24
36 = 24 × 1 + 12
24 = 12 × 2 + 0
Answer: 12

Example 4 — Use the HCF–LCM relation

The HCF of two numbers is 8 and their product is 3,840. Find their LCM.

LCM = product ÷ HCF = 3,840 ÷ 8.
Answer: 480

Example 5 — Co-prime numbers

Find the HCF and LCM of 9 and 16.

9 and 16 have no common factor other than 1.
HCF = 1; LCM = 9 × 16.
Answer: HCF = 1, LCM = 144

Example 6 — Three numbers

Find the HCF of 18, 30 and 42.

Factors common to all three include 1, 2 and 3.
The greatest common factor is 2 × 3.
Answer: 6

Example 7 — Bells ringing together

Three bells ring at intervals of 6, 8 and 12 minutes. If they ring together now, after how many minutes will they next ring together?

Find LCM(6, 8, 12).
6 = 2 × 3; 8 = 2³; 12 = 2² × 3.
LCM = 2³ × 3 = 24.
Answer: 24 minutes

Example 8 — Largest equal groups

48 red beads and 60 blue beads are to be divided into identical groups, with no beads left over. What is the greatest possible number of groups?

The number of groups must divide both 48 and 60.
HCF(48, 60) = 12.
Answer: 12 groups

Example 9 — Same remainder

Find the greatest number that divides 83 and 127 leaving the same remainder in each case.

If the remainders are equal, the divisor must divide the difference.
127 − 83 = 44.
The greatest possible divisor is 44.
Answer: 44 (remainder 39)

Example 10 — Smallest number divisible by several numbers

Find the smallest positive number exactly divisible by 4, 6 and 10.

4 = 2²; 6 = 2 × 3; 10 = 2 × 5.
LCM = 2² × 3 × 5 = 60.
Answer: 60
