Nutrition and Vitamins

RRB Group D General Science · Biology · Chapter 29

About this chapter

Nutrition supplies energy and raw materials needed for growth, repair and regulation of body processes.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Carbohydrates are a major energy source; proteins supply amino acids for growth and repair; fats provide concentrated energy and help absorb fat-soluble vitamins.
  2. Vitamins are organic micronutrients needed in small quantities. They do not directly provide energy but support metabolic functions.
  3. Fat-soluble vitamins are A, D, E and K; water-soluble vitamins include B-complex and C. Excess fat-soluble vitamins can accumulate.
  4. Vitamin A supports vision and epithelial health; deficiency can cause night blindness and, in severe cases, xerophthalmia.
  5. Vitamin B₁ (thiamine) deficiency can cause beriberi; vitamin C deficiency causes scurvy; vitamin D deficiency can cause rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults.
  6. Vitamin K is important for blood clotting; vitamin E functions as an antioxidant. Vitamin B₁₂ is needed for red blood cell formation and nerve function.
  7. Minerals include calcium for bones and teeth, iron for haemoglobin, iodine for thyroid hormones and sodium/potassium for fluid and nerve function.
  8. A balanced diet varies with age, activity and health. Dietary fibre supports bowel function; water is essential for transport and temperature regulation.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • fat-soluble vitamins: A, D, E, K
  • water-soluble: B group and C
