Nervous System and Coordination

RRB Group D General Science · Biology · Chapter 28

About this chapter

The nervous system detects stimuli, processes information and coordinates rapid responses.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. The central nervous system consists of brain and spinal cord. The peripheral nervous system connects the CNS to the rest of the body.
  2. A neuron is a specialised cell. Dendrites receive signals, the cell body integrates them and the axon conducts impulses away.
  3. A synapse is a junction between neurons or between a neuron and an effector; chemical neurotransmitters commonly transmit signals across it.
  4. The cerebrum is involved in conscious thought, memory, sensory processing and voluntary movement.
  5. The cerebellum coordinates movement, posture and balance. The brainstem controls vital functions such as breathing and heart rate.
  6. The spinal cord conducts signals and coordinates many reflexes. A reflex arc produces a rapid, automatic response.
  7. The autonomic nervous system regulates involuntary functions; sympathetic activity supports fight-or-flight and parasympathetic activity supports rest-and-digest.
  8. Endocrine glands release hormones into blood. Hormonal responses are often slower but longer-lasting than nerve impulses.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • reflex arc: receptor → sensory neuron → CNS → motor neuron → effector
