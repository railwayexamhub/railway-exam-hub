Plant Biology

RRB Group D General Science · Biology · Chapter 31

About this chapter

Plants are multicellular eukaryotes; most green plants make organic food by photosynthesis.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Roots anchor plants and absorb water and mineral ions. Root hairs increase the surface area for absorption.
  2. Stems support leaves and transport materials. Xylem carries water and minerals mainly upward; phloem transports sugars and other organic substances.
  3. Leaves are major photosynthetic organs. Stomata allow gas exchange; guard cells regulate their opening and closing.
  4. Photosynthesis uses light energy, chlorophyll, carbon dioxide and water to form carbohydrates and release oxygen.
  5. Transpiration is loss of water vapour from aerial plant parts, mainly through stomata. It helps pull water through xylem and cools leaves.
  6. Pollination is transfer of pollen from anther to stigma; fertilisation is fusion of male and female gametes to form a zygote.
  7. A seed contains an embryo and stored food or cotyledons; germination requires suitable water, oxygen and temperature.
  8. Plant hormones include auxins (growth and tropisms), gibberellins (stem growth and germination), cytokinins (cell division), abscisic acid (stress and dormancy) and ethylene (fruit ripening).

2. Important formulas and facts

  • photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
  • xylem: water/minerals; phloem: food
