Digestive System

RRB Group D General Science · Biology · Chapter 24

About this chapter

Digestion breaks large food molecules into smaller soluble molecules that can be absorbed and used by cells.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. The alimentary canal includes mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, rectum and anus.
  2. Mechanical digestion begins with chewing. Salivary amylase starts starch digestion in the mouth.
  3. The stomach churns food and secretes hydrochloric acid and enzymes; acid helps provide a suitable environment and kills many microbes.
  4. Bile is produced by the liver and stored in the gallbladder. It emulsifies fats but is not itself a digestive enzyme.
  5. The pancreas releases digestive enzymes and bicarbonate into the small intestine. The small intestine is the main site of digestion and nutrient absorption.
  6. Villi and microvilli increase the surface area of the small intestine for absorption. Blood capillaries absorb many nutrients; lacteals absorb much dietary fat.
  7. The large intestine absorbs water and salts and houses gut microbes; faeces are stored in the rectum before elimination.
  8. A balanced diet contains carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, fibre and water in appropriate amounts.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • starch digestion begins: salivary amylase
  • protein digestion begins mainly in stomach: pepsin
  • bile: fat emulsification
