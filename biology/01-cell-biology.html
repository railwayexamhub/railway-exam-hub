Cell Biology

RRB Group D General Science · Biology · Chapter 22

About this chapter

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life; organisms may be unicellular or multicellular.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Cell theory states that living organisms are made of cells, the cell is the basic unit of life, and cells arise from pre-existing cells.
  2. Prokaryotic cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bound organelles; bacteria are prokaryotes. Eukaryotic cells have a true nucleus.
  3. The plasma membrane is selectively permeable and regulates movement of substances. Diffusion and osmosis are important transport processes.
  4. The nucleus contains DNA and controls many cell activities. Chromosomes carry genes, which are units of heredity.
  5. Mitochondria are major sites of aerobic respiration and ATP production. Ribosomes synthesise proteins.
  6. Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll and carry out photosynthesis in plants and algae. The cell wall provides support in plants.
  7. The endoplasmic reticulum helps synthesise and transport proteins or lipids; the Golgi apparatus modifies, sorts and packages materials.
  8. Plant cells commonly have a cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts in photosynthetic tissues and a large central vacuole; animal cells lack a cell wall.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (light, chlorophyll)
  • ATP = cellular energy carrier
