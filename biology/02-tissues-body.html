Human Body and Tissues

RRB Group D General Science · Biology · Chapter 23

About this chapter

A tissue is a group of similar cells working together to perform a particular function.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. The four basic animal tissue types are epithelial, connective, muscular and nervous tissue.
  2. Epithelial tissue covers body surfaces and lines cavities; it protects, absorbs and secretes. It may be simple or stratified.
  3. Connective tissue supports and connects structures. Examples include bone, cartilage, blood, adipose tissue, tendons and ligaments.
  4. Muscle tissue contracts to produce movement. Skeletal muscle is usually voluntary and striated; smooth muscle is involuntary; cardiac muscle forms the heart.
  5. Nervous tissue contains neurons that transmit impulses and supporting glial cells. Neurons have a cell body, dendrites and an axon.
  6. Organs contain different tissues working together. Organ systems coordinate major functions such as digestion, respiration and circulation.
  7. Bone provides support, protection and mineral storage; cartilage provides flexible support and reduces friction at joints.
  8. Tendons connect muscle to bone; ligaments connect bone to bone. These are common exam distinctions.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • tendon: muscle-to-bone
  • ligament: bone-to-bone
