Excretory System

RRB Group D General Science · Biology · Chapter 27

About this chapter

Excretion removes metabolic wastes and helps regulate water, salts and acid-base balance.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. The urinary system includes two kidneys, two ureters, urinary bladder and urethra.
  2. The nephron is the functional unit of the kidney. It includes a glomerulus, Bowman's capsule and renal tubule.
  3. Ultrafiltration occurs at the glomerulus; useful substances and much water are reabsorbed along the tubule; secretion adds selected substances to the filtrate.
  4. Urine commonly contains water, urea, salts and other dissolved wastes. Healthy urine normally contains little or no glucose or protein.
  5. Urea is produced in the liver from ammonia generated during amino-acid metabolism and is carried by blood to the kidneys.
  6. The kidneys help maintain blood volume, electrolyte balance and pH. Antidiuretic hormone (ADH) influences water reabsorption.
  7. The lungs excrete carbon dioxide and water vapour; skin excretes small amounts of water, salts and urea in sweat.
  8. Dialysis can remove wastes and excess fluid from blood when kidneys fail, but it does not replace every kidney function.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • nephron = functional unit of kidney
  • urea formed in liver
  • urine pathway: kidney → ureter → bladder → urethra
