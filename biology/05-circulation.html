Circulatory System and Blood

RRB Group D General Science · Biology · Chapter 26

About this chapter

The circulatory system transports gases, nutrients, hormones and wastes around the body.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. The human heart has four chambers: right and left atria and right and left ventricles. Valves help prevent backflow.
  2. The right side pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs through the pulmonary artery; the left side pumps oxygenated blood to the body through the aorta.
  3. Pulmonary circulation is between heart and lungs; systemic circulation is between heart and the rest of the body.
  4. Arteries carry blood away from the heart and generally have thick elastic walls. Veins return blood to the heart and often contain valves.
  5. Capillaries have very thin walls and allow exchange of gases, nutrients and wastes with tissues.
  6. Red blood cells contain haemoglobin and transport oxygen. White blood cells defend against pathogens. Platelets help blood clot.
  7. Plasma is the liquid component of blood and transports nutrients, hormones, proteins, salts and waste products.
  8. The pulse is the pressure wave produced by each heartbeat in arteries. Blood pressure is commonly recorded as systolic over diastolic pressure.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • heart → lungs: pulmonary circulation
  • heart → body: systemic circulation
