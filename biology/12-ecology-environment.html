Ecology and Environment

RRB Group D General Science · Biology · Chapter 33

About this chapter

Ecology studies relationships among organisms and between organisms and their physical environment.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. An ecosystem includes a community of organisms and the abiotic environment interacting as a system.
  2. Producers such as green plants capture energy; consumers obtain energy by eating organisms; decomposers break down dead material and recycle nutrients.
  3. A food chain shows a single pathway of energy transfer; food webs show interconnected feeding relationships.
  4. Energy transfer between trophic levels is inefficient; much energy is lost as heat and through metabolism. The 10% rule is a rough ecological approximation.
  5. Biotic factors include living organisms; abiotic factors include light, temperature, water, soil and air.
  6. Biodiversity is variety of life at genetic, species and ecosystem levels. Habitat loss, invasive species, pollution, overexploitation and climate change threaten it.
  7. The carbon cycle moves carbon among atmosphere, organisms, oceans and rocks through photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition and combustion.
  8. Conservation includes protected areas, habitat restoration, sustainable use, pollution reduction and community participation.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • energy transfer: roughly 10% passed to next trophic level (approximation)
  • producers → consumers → decomposers
