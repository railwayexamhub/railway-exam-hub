Respiratory System

RRB Group D General Science · Biology · Chapter 25

About this chapter

Respiration is the process by which cells release usable energy from food; breathing moves air into and out of the lungs.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. The human respiratory pathway includes nasal cavity, pharynx, larynx, trachea, bronchi, bronchioles and alveoli.
  2. Alveoli are tiny air sacs with thin moist walls and a rich capillary supply, providing a large surface area for gas exchange.
  3. Oxygen diffuses from alveolar air into blood; carbon dioxide diffuses from blood into alveoli down partial-pressure gradients.
  4. The diaphragm contracts and flattens during inhalation, increasing thoracic volume and drawing air into the lungs.
  5. During exhalation at rest, the diaphragm relaxes and thoracic volume decreases, helping air move out.
  6. Most oxygen is transported bound to haemoglobin in red blood cells. Much carbon dioxide is transported as bicarbonate ions in plasma.
  7. Aerobic cellular respiration uses oxygen to release energy from glucose, producing carbon dioxide and water; ATP captures usable energy.
  8. Smoking damages airways and alveoli and increases risk of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, lung cancer and cardiovascular disease.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • aerobic respiration: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy
  • alveoli: gas exchange
