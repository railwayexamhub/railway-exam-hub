Reproduction and Genetics

RRB Group D General Science · Biology · Chapter 32

About this chapter

Reproduction produces offspring; heredity passes genetic information from parents to offspring.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Asexual reproduction involves one parent and no fusion of gametes; offspring are often genetically similar to the parent, except for mutations.
  2. Sexual reproduction involves formation and fusion of gametes. In humans, fertilisation usually occurs in the oviduct and the embryo implants in the uterus.
  3. In humans, sperm are produced in testes and ova in ovaries. The placenta enables exchange of nutrients, gases and wastes between maternal and fetal blood without normally mixing them directly.
  4. DNA stores hereditary information. A gene is a DNA sequence that contributes to a functional product; chromosomes contain DNA and proteins.
  5. Mitosis produces two genetically similar daughter cells and supports growth and repair. Meiosis produces haploid cells and contributes to genetic variation.
  6. In humans, body cells typically have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs); gametes have 23. At fertilisation, the diploid number is restored.
  7. Gregor Mendel's pea plant experiments established basic principles of inheritance, including segregation of alleles.
  8. Sex determination in humans usually involves XX chromosomes in females and XY in males; the sperm contributes either X or Y.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • human somatic cells: 46 chromosomes
  • human gametes: 23 chromosomes
  • mitosis: 2 similar cells; meiosis: 4 haploid cells (typical outcome)
