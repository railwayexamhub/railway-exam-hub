Human Diseases and Immunity

RRB Group D General Science · Biology · Chapter 30

About this chapter

Disease may result from infectious agents, genetic factors, nutritional deficiencies, environmental exposures or lifestyle factors.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Pathogens include bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa and parasitic worms. Transmission may occur through air, water, food, vectors, contact or body fluids.
  2. Bacteria are cellular organisms; viruses are acellular infectious agents that replicate inside host cells. Antibiotics act against bacteria, not viruses.
  3. Malaria is caused by Plasmodium and transmitted by female Anopheles mosquitoes. Dengue is transmitted mainly by Aedes mosquitoes.
  4. Tuberculosis is caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis and commonly affects lungs; it spreads through airborne particles from an infectious person.
  5. Vaccination exposes the immune system to antigens safely or in a controlled form, helping build immune memory and reduce severe disease.
  6. Innate immunity provides rapid, non-specific defence; adaptive immunity involves specific B and T lymphocytes and immunological memory.
  7. Antibodies are proteins produced by B-cell-derived plasma cells that bind specific antigens. T cells coordinate immune responses or kill infected cells.
  8. Prevention includes safe water, sanitation, vaccination, vector control, hand hygiene, ventilation and appropriate medical care.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • malaria: Plasmodium; vector: female Anopheles
  • TB: Mycobacterium tuberculosis
  • antibiotics do not treat viral infections
