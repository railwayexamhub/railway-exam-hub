Train Problems

RRB Group D Mathematics • Chapter 13

About this chapter

Train questions use relative speed and the distance covered while crossing an object or another train.

Study tip: Read the rules, practise the worked examples, then attempt the quiz without looking at the notes.

1. Key concepts and rules

  • To cross a pole, a train covers its own length.
  • To cross a platform, it covers train length plus platform length.
  • For opposite directions, add speeds.
  • For the same direction, subtract the slower speed from the faster speed.
  • Convert km/h to m/s for calculations involving metres and seconds.

2. Important formulas and reminders

Time to cross pole = Train length ÷ Speed
Time to cross platform = (Train length + Platform length) ÷ Speed
Relative speed, opposite directions = sum of speeds
Relative speed, same direction = difference of speeds

3. Solved examples

Example 1: A 120 m train passes a pole at 12 m/s. Find time.

Time = 120/12.

Answer: 10 seconds

Example 2: A 150 m train crosses a 250 m platform at 20 m/s.

Distance = 150+250 = 400 m; time = 400/20.

Answer: 20 seconds

Example 3: Two trains move opposite ways at 40 and 50 km/h. Find relative speed.

Opposite-direction speeds are added.

Answer: 90 km/h

4. Exam tips

  • Write down the given values before selecting a formula.
  • Check that units are consistent.
  • Do calculations step by step and simplify the final answer.
  • Estimate the result to catch arithmetic mistakes.
  • Practise questions under a time limit after learning the method.
