Number System

Key points

  • Divisibility: by 2 (last digit even), 3 (digit sum divisible by 3), 4 (last two digits), 5 (ends 0 or 5), 6 (by 2 and 3), 8 (last three digits), 9 (digit sum divisible by 9), 11 (difference of sum of odd-place and even-place digits is 0 or 11).
  • Sum of first n natural numbers = n(n+1)/2. Sum of first n odd numbers = n². Sum of first n even numbers = n(n+1).
  • Unit digits repeat in cycles: 2, 3, 7 and 8 repeat every 4 powers; 4 and 9 repeat every 2; 0, 1, 5 and 6 never change.
  • Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder.

Worked example

Is 7425 divisible by 9?
Digit sum is 7+4+2+5 = 18, which is divisible by 9, so yes.

Practice questions

  1. Find the unit digit of 7²⁵.
    Show answer

    7. The cycle of 7 is 7, 9, 3, 1 (length 4) and 25 leaves remainder 1, so the unit digit is 7.

  2. Sum of the first 30 natural numbers?
    Show answer

    30×31/2 = 465.

BODMAS and Simplification

Key points

  • Order: Brackets (innermost first), Orders (powers and roots), Division and Multiplication (left to right), Addition and Subtraction (left to right).
  • Division and multiplication have equal rank, so work from left to right. The same applies to addition and subtraction.
  • Use (a+b)², a²−b² and similar identities to avoid long multiplication.

Worked example

Simplify 8 + 12 ÷ 4 × 3 − 2.
12 ÷ 4 = 3, then 3 × 3 = 9, so 8 + 9 − 2 = 15.

Practice questions

  1. 5 × 6 − 12 ÷ 3 + 2 = ?
    Show answer

    30 − 4 + 2 = 28.

  2. 100 − [20 + (15 − 5) × 2] = ?
    Show answer

    (15−5)=10, ×2 = 20, 20+20 = 40, 100−40 = 60.

Fractions and Decimals

Key points

  • To add or subtract fractions, make the denominators equal using the LCM.
  • To compare fractions, cross-multiply or convert to decimals.
  • Recurring decimal 0.ab (bar over ab) = ab/99. Example: 0.45 recurring = 45/99 = 5/11.
  • Terminating decimal as fraction: write over a power of 10 and reduce. 0.45 = 45/100 = 9/20.

Worked example

Add 3/4 + 5/6.
LCM of 4 and 6 is 12. 3/4 = 9/12 and 5/6 = 10/12, so the sum is 19/12.

Practice questions

  1. Write 0.45 as a fraction in lowest terms.
    Show answer

    45/100 = 9/20.

  2. Find 2/3 of 450.
    Show answer

    450 ÷ 3 × 2 = 300.

LCM and HCF

Key points

  • LCM × HCF = product of the two numbers.
  • HCF is the largest number that divides all given numbers. LCM is the smallest number divisible by all of them.
  • Use LCM for events that repeat together (bells, traffic lights). Use HCF for the largest equal pieces or groups.
  • HCF of fractions = HCF of numerators ÷ LCM of denominators.

Worked example

The HCF of two numbers is 12 and their LCM is 180. One number is 36. Find the other.
Other = (12 × 180) ÷ 36 = 60.

Practice questions

  1. LCM of 12, 18 and 24?
    Show answer

    72.

  2. Largest number that divides both 60 and 84 exactly?
    Show answer

    HCF = 12.

  3. Three bells ring every 6, 8 and 12 minutes and ring together at 9:00. When do they next ring together?
    Show answer

    LCM = 24 minutes, so at 9:24.

Ratio and Proportion

Key points

  • If a:b = c:d, then ad = bc.
  • To divide an amount in ratio a:b:c, divide by (a+b+c) and multiply by each part.
  • Mean proportional of a and b = √(ab). Third proportional of a and b is b²/a.
  • Partnership: profit is shared in the ratio of capital × time.

Worked example

Divide 840 in the ratio 3:4:5.
Total parts = 12, one part = 70. Shares are 210, 280 and 350.

Practice questions

  1. A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5. Find A:B:C.
    Show answer

    Make B equal: A:B = 8:12 and B:C = 12:15, so A:B:C = 8:12:15.

  2. Ages are in the ratio 4:5 and sum to 45. Find the ages.
    Show answer

    One part = 5. Ages are 20 and 25.

Percentage

Key points

  • x% means x/100. To find x% of N, compute N × x/100.
  • Useful values: 1/2 = 50%, 1/3 = 33.33%, 1/4 = 25%, 1/5 = 20%, 1/6 = 16.67%, 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/10 = 10%.
  • Successive change of a% and b%: net change = a + b + (ab/100).
  • If A is r% more than B, then B is r/(100+r) × 100 % less than A.

Worked example

Find 20% of 350.
350 × 20/100 = 70.

Practice questions

  1. A price rises by 20% and then falls by 20%. What is the net change?
    Show answer

    20 − 20 − (400/100) = −4%, a 4% decrease.

  2. A's salary is 25% more than B's. B's salary is what percent less than A's?
    Show answer

    25/125 × 100 = 20%.

Profit, Loss and Discount

Key points

  • Profit = SP − CP. Profit% = (Profit/CP) × 100. Loss% = (Loss/CP) × 100.
  • SP = CP × (100 + P)/100 for profit, and CP × (100 − L)/100 for loss.
  • Discount is always on the marked price: SP = MP × (100 − d)/100.
  • Two successive discounts a% and b% equal a + b − (ab/100) in total.

Worked example

CP is ₹500 and SP is ₹575. Find the profit percent.
Profit = 75. Profit% = 75/500 × 100 = 15%.

Practice questions

  1. Marked price ₹800, discount 15%. Find the selling price.
    Show answer

    800 × 85/100 = ₹680.

  2. What single discount equals successive discounts of 10% and 20%?
    Show answer

    10 + 20 − 2 = 28%.

Simple and Compound Interest

Key points

  • Simple interest SI = (P × R × T)/100. Amount A = P + SI.
  • Compound amount A = P(1 + R/100)ᵀ. CI = A − P.
  • For 2 years: CI − SI = P(R/100)². For 3 years the difference is P(R/100)²(3 + R/100).
  • If interest compounds half-yearly, halve the rate and double the number of periods.

Worked example

Find the SI on ₹5000 at 8% per year for 3 years.
SI = 5000 × 8 × 3 / 100 = ₹1200.

Practice questions

  1. Find the CI on ₹10,000 at 10% for 2 years.
    Show answer

    Amount = 10000 × 1.1 × 1.1 = 12100, so CI = ₹2100.

  2. Find the difference between CI and SI on ₹2000 at 5% for 2 years.
    Show answer

    2000 × (5/100)² = ₹5.

Average

Key points

  • Average = sum of observations ÷ number of observations.
  • Sum = average × number of observations. Use this to find a missing value.
  • If a person joins or leaves, find the change in total sum and divide by the new count.
  • Average of first n natural numbers = (n+1)/2.

Worked example

The average of 5 numbers is 20. A sixth number 38 is added. Find the new average.
Old sum = 100. New sum = 138. New average = 138/6 = 23.

Practice questions

  1. Average of the first 10 natural numbers?
    Show answer

    (10+1)/2 = 5.5.

  2. The average of 6 numbers is 40. When one number is removed, the average of the rest is 38. Find the removed number.
    Show answer

    240 − (38 × 5) = 240 − 190 = 50.

Time and Work

Key points

  • If A does a job in a days, A's one-day work is 1/a.
  • A and B together take ab/(a+b) days.
  • Efficiency method: assume total work as the LCM of the days, then find each person's units per day.
  • Work done is proportional to men × days × hours.

Worked example

A takes 10 days and B takes 15 days. How long together?
10 × 15 / (10 + 15) = 150/25 = 6 days.

Practice questions

  1. A takes 12 days and B takes 24 days. How long together?
    Show answer

    12 × 24 / 36 = 8 days.

  2. A can do a job in 20 days. He works for 5 days and leaves. How many days would A alone need for the rest?
    Show answer

    5 days is 1/4 of the work, so the rest takes 15 days.

Pipes and Cisterns

Key points

  • An inlet pipe fills a tank. An outlet pipe or leak empties it.
  • Rate of filling = 1/time. Net rate = sum of inlet rates − sum of outlet rates.
  • Time to fill = 1 ÷ net rate.
  • Use the LCM of times as the tank capacity to make the arithmetic easier.

Worked example

Pipe A fills a tank in 12 hours and pipe B in 15 hours. How long together?
12 × 15 / 27 = 20/3 hours = 6 hours 40 minutes.

Practice questions

  1. A fills a tank in 6 hours and a leak empties it in 12 hours. How long to fill?
    Show answer

    1/6 − 1/12 = 1/12, so 12 hours.

  2. Pipes A and B fill a tank in 10 and 15 hours and pipe C empties it in 30 hours. All are open. How long to fill?
    Show answer

    1/10 + 1/15 − 1/30 = 4/30, so 7.5 hours.

Time, Speed and Distance

Key points

  • Speed = Distance ÷ Time.
  • To convert km/h to m/s multiply by 5/18. To convert m/s to km/h multiply by 18/5.
  • If distance is the same, average speed = 2xy/(x+y).
  • Boats: downstream speed = boat + stream. Upstream speed = boat − stream.

Worked example

Convert 72 km/h to m/s.
72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s.

Practice questions

  1. A car covers 60 km at 30 km/h and returns the same 60 km at 60 km/h. What is the average speed for the whole trip?
    Show answer

    2 × 30 × 60 / 90 = 40 km/h.

  2. A boat's speed in still water is 10 km/h and the stream is 2 km/h. Find the upstream speed.
    Show answer

    10 − 2 = 8 km/h.

Train Problems

Key points

  • Crossing a pole or a person: distance = length of train.
  • Crossing a platform or bridge: distance = length of train + length of platform.
  • Two trains in opposite directions: relative speed = sum of speeds. Same direction: difference.
  • Time = total distance ÷ relative speed. Convert km/h to m/s first.

Worked example

A 150 m train travels at 54 km/h. How long to cross a pole?
54 km/h = 15 m/s. Time = 150/15 = 10 seconds.

Practice questions

  1. A 200 m train at 72 km/h crosses a 300 m platform. How long does it take?
    Show answer

    72 km/h = 20 m/s. Time = 500/20 = 25 seconds.

  2. Two trains 100 m and 120 m long run in opposite directions at 40 km/h and 50 km/h. How long to cross each other?
    Show answer

    Relative speed = 90 km/h = 25 m/s. Distance = 220 m. Time = 8.8 seconds.

Mensuration

Key points

  • Rectangle: area = l × b, perimeter = 2(l + b). Square: area = a², perimeter = 4a.
  • Triangle: area = ½ × base × height. Circle: area = πr², circumference = 2πr.
  • Cuboid: volume = lbh, total surface area = 2(lb + bh + hl). Cube: volume = a³, surface area = 6a².
  • Cylinder: volume = πr²h, curved surface = 2πrh. Cone: volume = ⅓πr²h. Sphere: surface = 4πr², volume = 4/3 πr³.

Worked example

Find the area of a circle of radius 7 cm (π = 22/7).
22/7 × 7 × 7 = 154 cm².

Practice questions

  1. A cube has volume 27 cm³. Find its total surface area.
    Show answer

    Side = 3 cm. Surface area = 6 × 9 = 54 cm².

  2. Find the volume of a cylinder with r = 7 cm and h = 10 cm.
    Show answer

    22/7 × 49 × 10 = 1540 cm³.

Geometry

Key points

  • Angles in a triangle add up to 180°. An exterior angle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles.
  • Angles in a quadrilateral add up to 360°. Interior angle sum of an n-sided polygon = (n − 2) × 180°.
  • Pythagoras: in a right triangle, hypotenuse² = base² + height².
  • Angle in a semicircle is 90°. Angles in the same segment are equal.

Worked example

Two angles of a triangle are 50° and 60°. Find the third.
180 − 110 = 70°.

Practice questions

  1. Find each interior angle of a regular hexagon.
    Show answer

    (6 − 2) × 180 / 6 = 120°.

  2. The legs of a right triangle are 6 cm and 8 cm. Find the hypotenuse.
    Show answer

    √(36 + 64) = 10 cm.

Algebra

Key points

  • (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b². (a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b². a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b).
  • a³ + b³ = (a + b)(a² − ab + b²). a³ − b³ = (a − b)(a² + ab + b²).
  • If x + 1/x = k, then x² + 1/x² = k² − 2.
  • For a linear equation, bring the variable terms to one side and constants to the other.

Worked example

If x + y = 10 and xy = 21, find x² + y².
x² + y² = (x + y)² − 2xy = 100 − 42 = 58.

Practice questions

  1. Find 105² − 95².
    Show answer

    (105 + 95)(105 − 95) = 200 × 10 = 2000.

  2. Solve 3x + 5 = 20.
    Show answer

    3x = 15, so x = 5.

Trigonometry

Key points

  • sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. tanθ = sinθ/cosθ.
  • Values: sin 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90° = 0, 1/2, 1/√2, √3/2, 1. cos is the same list in reverse. tan = 0, 1/√3, 1, √3, undefined.
  • In a right triangle: sinθ = opposite/hypotenuse, cosθ = adjacent/hypotenuse, tanθ = opposite/adjacent.
  • sin(90° − θ) = cosθ and cos(90° − θ) = sinθ.

Worked example

Find sin 30° + cos 60°.
1/2 + 1/2 = 1.

Practice questions

  1. Find sin²45° + cos²45°.
    Show answer

    It equals 1 for any angle.

  2. Find tan 45° × sin 90°.
    Show answer

    1 × 1 = 1.

  3. If sinθ = 3/5, find cosθ (θ is acute).
    Show answer

    cosθ = √(1 − 9/25) = 4/5.

Statistics and Data Interpretation

Key points

  • Mean = sum ÷ count. Median is the middle value of sorted data (average of the two middle values if the count is even). Mode is the most frequent value.
  • Range = highest value − lowest value.
  • Empirical relation: Mode = 3 × Median − 2 × Mean.
  • For tables and graphs, read the title, units and scale first, then calculate.

Worked example

Find the mean and median of 3, 5, 7, 9, 11.
Mean = 35/5 = 7. Median = middle value = 7.

Practice questions

  1. Find the median of 4, 8, 2, 10, 6, 12.
    Show answer

    Sorted: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12. Median = (6 + 8)/2 = 7.

  2. Find the mean and mode of 2, 4, 4, 6.
    Show answer

    Mean = 16/4 = 4. Mode = 4.

Age Problems

Key points

  • Let the present ages be variables, then write each statement as an equation.
  • After n years add n to each person's age. n years ago subtract n.
  • The difference between two people's ages never changes.
  • If ages are in ratio a:b, take them as ax and bx.

Worked example

A father is 3 times as old as his son. After 10 years he will be 2 times as old. Find their present ages.
Son = x, father = 3x. 3x + 10 = 2(x + 10), so x = 10. Son is 10 and father is 30.

Practice questions

  1. The sum of two ages is 50 and the ratio is 2:3. Find the ages.
    Show answer

    One part = 10. Ages are 20 and 30.

  2. Five years ago a father was 4 times as old as his son. Their present ages add up to 50. Find the present ages.
    Show answer

    Let son = s, father = 50 − s. 50 − s − 5 = 4(s − 5), so s = 13. Son is 13 and father is 37.

Calendar and Clock

Key points

  • Odd days: an ordinary year has 1 odd day and a leap year has 2. 100 years have 5 odd days, 200 years have 3, 300 years have 1 and 400 years have 0.
  • A year is a leap year if divisible by 4. A century year is a leap year only if divisible by 400.
  • Angle between clock hands = |30H − 5.5M| degrees, where H is the hour and M is the minutes.
  • The hands of a clock overlap 11 times in 12 hours.

Worked example

Find the angle between the hands at 3:00.
|30 × 3 − 0| = 90°.

Practice questions

  1. Find the angle between the hands at 6:00.
    Show answer

    |180 − 0| = 180°.

  2. Is 2100 a leap year?
    Show answer

    No. It is a century year not divisible by 400.

  3. 1 January 2024 was a Monday. What day was 1 January 2025?
    Show answer

    2024 is a leap year with 2 odd days, so Wednesday.
