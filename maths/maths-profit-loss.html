Maths: Profit & Loss and Discount

RRB Group D / NTPC / ALP / JE • Concept, tricks, solved examples, practice

1. Formulas & concepts

  • Profit = SP − CP; Loss = CP − SP
  • Profit % = (Profit/CP)×100; Loss % = (Loss/CP)×100
  • SP = CP × (100 ± p)/100; CP = SP × 100/(100 ± p)
  • Discount = MP − SP; Discount % = (Discount/MP)×100
  • Successive discounts a%, b%: net = a + b − ab/100
  • Marked price MP = CP × (100 + p)/(100 − d)
  • Same SP, one item +x% and the other −x%: net loss = x²/100 %
  • False weight: gain % = error/(true weight − error) × 100

2. Memory tricks

  • Profit and loss are on CP, discount is on MP. The base is different, never mix them.
  • CP = 100 trick: if CP is not given, assume 100. Then profit % = profit.
  • Multiplier method: 20% profit = ×1.2, 10% discount = ×0.9. Chain: 1.2 × 0.9 = 1.08, so 8% profit.
  • Faulty weight: selling 900 g as 1 kg gives gain = 100/900 = 11.11%.
  • Discount + profit: write MP as 100, SP = 100 − d, CP = SP/(1 + p) and compare.

3. Solved examples

Ex 1. CP = ₹500, SP = ₹575. Find profit %.
Profit = 75, so 75/500 × 100 = 15%.
Ex 2. An article marked ₹1200 is sold at 15% discount and gives 20% profit. Find CP.
SP = 1200 × 0.85 = 1020. CP = 1020/1.2 = ₹850.
Ex 3. Two successive discounts of 20% and 10%: single equivalent discount?
20 + 10 − (20×10)/100 = 28%.

4. Practice (tap to see answer)

Q1. CP = ₹250, loss 12%. Find SP.

(a) 210 (b) 220 (c) 230 (d) 240

Answer: (b) ₹220

Q2. SP = ₹900 with 20% profit. CP = ?

(a) 700 (b) 720 (c) 750 (d) 780

Answer: (c) ₹750

Q3. A 25% gain on CP means CP is what % of SP?

(a) 75% (b) 80% (c) 85% (d) 90%

Answer: (b) 80%

Q4. Two successive discounts of 10% and 10%. Net discount?

(a) 20% (b) 19% (c) 18% (d) 21%

Answer: (b) 19%

Q5. Two items sold at ₹540 each, one at 20% gain and one at 20% loss. Overall result?

(a) no loss (b) 2% loss (c) 4% loss (d) 4% gain

Answer: (c) 4% loss (x²/100 = 400/100)

5. Expanded lesson: Marked price and successive discounts

Profit and loss percentages are calculated on cost price. Discount percentage is calculated on marked price. For successive discounts a% and b%, equivalent discount = a+b−ab/100.
Additional solved example 1.
An article costing ₹750 is sold for ₹900. Profit percentage?
Solution: Profit=₹150; 150/750×100=20%.
Additional solved example 2.
Find the equivalent discount for 20% followed by 5%.
Solution: 20+5−(20×5/100)=24%.
