Units and Measurements

RRB Group D General Science · Physics Chapter 1

About this chapter

Physics describes the natural world using measurable quantities. To measure a quantity, we compare it with an agreed standard called a unit. This chapter introduces physical quantities, SI units, measuring instruments and common conversions.

Learning goals: Identify base and derived quantities, remember the seven SI base units, select suitable measuring instruments and solve simple unit conversions.

Exam focus: Memorise unit names and symbols, distinguish mass from weight, and practise conversions such as kilometre to metre and hour to second.

1. Physical quantities and measurement

A physical quantity is a property that can be measured and expressed by a number and a unit. For example, if a table is 2 metres long, 2 is the numerical value and metre (m) is the unit.

Base (fundamental) quantities

Base quantities are independent quantities. Their units are not expressed in terms of other base units.

Derived quantities

Derived quantities are formed by combining base quantities mathematically.

6. Mass and weight

MassWeight
Amount of matter in an object.Gravitational force acting on an object.
SI unit: kilogram (kg).SI unit: newton (N).
Does not change with location in ordinary situations.Can change when gravitational acceleration changes.
Measured using a balance.Can be measured using a spring balance.

Formula: Weight = mass × acceleration due to gravity

W = mg, where W is in newtons, m is in kilograms and g is in m/s². Near Earth's surface, g is approximately 9.8 m/s².

7. Solved examples

Example 1 – Convert 3.5 km into metres

3.5 × 1,000 = 3,500 m.

Answer: 3,500 metres.

Example 2 – Convert 2 hours into seconds

2 × 3,600 = 7,200 seconds.

Answer: 7,200 seconds.

Example 3 – Convert 72 km/h into m/s

72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s.

Answer: 20 m/s.

Example 4 – Find density

Mass = 6 kg; volume = 2 m³. Density = 6 ÷ 2 = 3 kg/m³.

Answer: 3 kg/m³.

Example 5 – Find weight

Mass = 5 kg; g = 9.8 m/s². W = mg = 5 × 9.8 = 49 N.

Answer: 49 N.

Example 6 – Convert 0.75 m into centimetres

0.75 × 100 = 75 cm.

Answer: 75 cm.

8. Quick revision

  • Measurement = numerical value + unit.
  • SI has seven base units.
  • Metre measures length; kilogram measures mass; second measures time.
  • Area is measured in m² and volume in m³.
  • Speed is measured in m/s; acceleration in m/s².
  • Force is measured in newtons (N).
  • Vernier calipers measure internal/external dimensions and depth.
  • A screw gauge measures very small thickness or diameter.
  • Mass and weight are different physical quantities.
  • Always write the correct unit with a numerical answer.
