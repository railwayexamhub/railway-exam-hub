Pipes and Cisterns

RRB Group D Mathematics • Chapter 11

About this chapter

Pipes and cisterns use the same rate method as time and work. Inlet pipes fill; outlet pipes empty.

Study tip: Read the rules, practise the worked examples, then attempt the quiz without looking at the notes.

1. Key concepts and rules

  • An inlet has a positive filling rate.
  • An outlet has a negative emptying rate.
  • When pipes work together, add signed rates.
  • If a pipe fills a tank in x hours, its hourly rate is 1/x tank.

2. Important formulas and reminders

Inlet rate = +1/x tank per hour
Outlet rate = −1/y tank per hour
Net rate = sum of all signed rates
Filling time = 1 ÷ net positive rate

3. Solved examples

Example 1: A pipe fills a tank in 4 hours. What fraction does it fill in one hour?

Rate = 1/4 tank per hour.

Answer: 1/4

Example 2: An inlet fills in 6 hours and an outlet empties in 12 hours. Find net filling time.

Net rate = 1/6−1/12 = 1/12 tank per hour.

Answer: 12 hours

Example 3: Two inlets fill a tank in 3 and 6 hours. Find time together.

Rate = 1/3+1/6 = 1/2.

Answer: 2 hours

4. Exam tips

  • Write down the given values before selecting a formula.
  • Check that units are consistent.
  • Do calculations step by step and simplify the final answer.
  • Estimate the result to catch arithmetic mistakes.
  • Practise questions under a time limit after learning the method.
