Fuels and Combustion

RRB Group D General Science · Chemistry · Chapter 19

About this chapter

A fuel releases usable energy, usually as heat, through chemical or nuclear processes.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Combustion is a chemical reaction with an oxidant, commonly oxygen, releasing heat and often light.
  2. Ignition temperature is the minimum temperature at which a substance catches fire and continues burning under specified conditions.
  3. A fuel's calorific value is the heat released by complete combustion of a unit mass (or volume) of fuel.
  4. Ideal fuels are readily available, economical, have high calorific value, burn at a controllable rate and produce minimal pollution or residue.
  5. Fossil fuels include coal, petroleum and natural gas. They are non-renewable and combustion contributes to greenhouse gas emissions.
  6. LPG mainly contains propane and butane; CNG mainly contains methane. Both burn relatively cleanly compared with many solid fuels, but are still fossil-derived in common use.
  7. Fire requires fuel, oxygen and sufficient heat. Removing any one component can extinguish a fire. Different fire classes require suitable extinguishers.
  8. Carbon monoxide forms during incomplete combustion and binds strongly to haemoglobin, reducing oxygen transport; adequate ventilation is essential.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • calorific value = heat released/mass of fuel
  • complete hydrocarbon combustion → CO₂ + H₂O

3. Worked examples

A fuel releases 80,000 kJ from 2 kg. Find calorific value.

Answer: 40,000 kJ/kg

80,000 ÷ 2 = 40,000 kJ/kg.
