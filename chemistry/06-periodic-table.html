Periodic Classification of Elements

RRB Group D General Science · Chemistry · Chapter 17

About this chapter

The periodic table arranges elements so that properties show repeating patterns with increasing atomic number.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Modern periodic law: properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers.
  2. The modern periodic table has 7 periods (rows) and 18 groups (columns). Elements in a group often have similar valence-electron configurations and chemical properties.
  3. Across a period, atomic radius generally decreases while effective nuclear attraction increases; down a group, atomic radius generally increases.
  4. Metallic character generally decreases across a period and increases down a group. Non-metallic character shows the opposite broad trend.
  5. Group 1 elements are alkali metals; Group 2 are alkaline earth metals; Group 17 are halogens; Group 18 are noble gases.
  6. Noble gases have filled outer shells (helium has a duplet) and are generally unreactive. Halogens are reactive non-metals.
  7. The period number is related to the number of occupied electron shells for main-group elements. Group position helps indicate valence electrons.
  8. Hydrogen has a unique position because it shows similarities with both alkali metals and halogens.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • periods = 7
  • groups = 18
  • atomic number = proton number

3. Worked examples

How many groups are in the modern periodic table?

Answer: 18

The modern periodic table contains 18 vertical groups.
