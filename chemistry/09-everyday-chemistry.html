Chemistry in Everyday Life

RRB Group D General Science · Chemistry · Chapter 20

About this chapter

Chemistry explains materials and reactions used in food, cleaning, medicines, agriculture and household products.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Hard water contains dissolved calcium and magnesium ions. Temporary hardness is commonly associated with bicarbonates; permanent hardness with sulfates and chlorides.
  2. Soap reacts with calcium and magnesium ions to form insoluble scum. Detergents are formulated to remain effective in hard water.
  3. Bleaching agents are used for whitening and disinfection; they must be handled according to label directions and never mixed casually with acids or ammonia.
  4. Antacids neutralise excess stomach acid. Examples include magnesium hydroxide and calcium carbonate; medicines should be used as directed.
  5. Fertilisers supply essential plant nutrients, especially nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium (NPK). Excess runoff can cause eutrophication.
  6. Food preservatives slow spoilage by microorganisms or oxidation. Safe storage, hygiene and correct use limits are important.
  7. Polymers are large molecules made of repeating units. Examples include polyethylene, PVC, nylon and natural cellulose.
  8. Pesticides control pests but can harm non-target organisms; integrated pest management and safe handling reduce risks.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • hardness ions: Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺
  • NPK = nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium
