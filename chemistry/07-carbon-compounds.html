Carbon and Its Compounds

RRB Group D General Science · Chemistry · Chapter 18

About this chapter

Carbon forms a vast range of compounds because it is tetravalent and can bond to itself in chains and rings.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Carbon has atomic number 6 and four valence electrons; it commonly forms four covalent bonds.
  2. Catenation is carbon's ability to form bonds with other carbon atoms, producing chains, branches and rings.
  3. Saturated hydrocarbons contain only single carbon-carbon bonds (alkanes); unsaturated hydrocarbons contain double or triple bonds (alkenes and alkynes).
  4. Homologous series members share a functional group and general formula; successive members differ by –CH₂– and have gradually changing physical properties.
  5. Functional groups determine characteristic reactions: alcohol –OH, aldehyde –CHO, ketone >C=O, carboxylic acid –COOH and halogen –X.
  6. Combustion of hydrocarbons in sufficient oxygen produces carbon dioxide and water; incomplete combustion may produce carbon monoxide or soot.
  7. Ethanol is an alcohol; ethanoic acid is a carboxylic acid. Esterification of an alcohol with a carboxylic acid forms an ester and water.
  8. Soaps form scum in hard water; detergents generally work better in hard water. Soap molecules have hydrophilic and hydrophobic parts.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • alkane: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂
  • alkene: CₙH₂ₙ
  • alkyne: CₙH₂ₙ₋₂

3. Worked examples

What is the molecular formula of propane?

Answer: C₃H₈

For alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂; n = 3 gives C₃H₈.
