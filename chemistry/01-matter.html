Matter and Its Nature

RRB Group D General Science · Chemistry · Chapter 12

About this chapter

Matter has mass and occupies space. Its particles are in constant motion and attract one another.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Solids have a definite shape and volume; liquids have definite volume but take the shape of their container; gases have neither fixed shape nor fixed volume.
  2. Diffusion is mixing caused by random particle motion. It is generally faster in gases than liquids and faster at higher temperature.
  3. Melting changes solid to liquid; freezing is the reverse. Vaporisation changes liquid to gas; condensation changes gas to liquid; sublimation is direct solid-to-gas change.
  4. During a pure substance's change of state at fixed pressure, temperature remains constant while latent heat is absorbed or released.
  5. Evaporation occurs at the surface at temperatures below boiling and causes cooling because higher-energy particles escape.
  6. An element contains one type of atom; a compound contains two or more elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio; a mixture has variable composition.
  7. Filtration separates insoluble solids from liquids; evaporation recovers dissolved solids; distillation separates liquids by boiling points; chromatography separates components by different movement.
  8. Physical changes generally do not form new substances; chemical changes produce substances with different compositions and properties.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • density = mass/volume
  • Q = mL (latent heat)
  • temperature conversion: K = °C + 273.15

3. Worked examples

A 200 g sample occupies 50 cm³. Find density.

Answer: 4 g/cm³

Density = mass/volume = 200/50 = 4 g/cm³.
