Metals and Non-metals

RRB Group D General Science · Chemistry · Chapter 16

About this chapter

Elements are classified by characteristic physical and chemical properties; metals generally conduct heat and electricity and are malleable.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Metals are generally lustrous, malleable, ductile and good conductors. Exceptions exist: mercury is liquid at room temperature and some metals are relatively soft.
  2. Non-metals are generally poor conductors, brittle as solids and may be gases or solids; graphite is a notable conducting non-metal.
  3. Metals react with oxygen to form metal oxides, often basic; some oxides are amphoteric, reacting with both acids and bases.
  4. Many metals react with acids to produce salt and hydrogen, but less reactive metals such as copper do not liberate hydrogen from dilute non-oxidising acids.
  5. The reactivity series arranges metals by tendency to lose electrons. A more reactive metal can displace a less reactive metal from its salt solution.
  6. Ionic compounds form by electron transfer, commonly from metals to non-metals. They have high melting points and conduct electricity when molten or dissolved.
  7. Corrosion is gradual deterioration by chemical reaction with the environment. Painting, oiling, galvanising and alloying can protect iron.
  8. Alloys are mixtures designed for improved properties: brass is copper-zinc; bronze is copper-tin; stainless steel contains iron, chromium and often nickel.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • metal + acid → salt + hydrogen (for suitable reactive metals)
  • iron rust: hydrated iron(III) oxide
  • galvanising = zinc coating

3. Worked examples

Which metal is commonly used to galvanise iron?

Answer: Zinc

Zinc coating protects iron from corrosion.
