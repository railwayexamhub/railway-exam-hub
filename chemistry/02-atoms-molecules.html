Atoms and Molecules

RRB Group D General Science · Chemistry · Chapter 13

About this chapter

Atoms are the basic units of elements that take part in chemical changes; molecules are groups of atoms bonded together.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. An atom contains a nucleus of protons and neutrons, surrounded by electrons. Protons are positive, electrons negative and neutrons neutral.
  2. Atomic number Z equals the number of protons. In a neutral atom, electrons equal protons.
  3. Mass number A is the total number of protons and neutrons. Neutrons = A − Z.
  4. Isotopes have the same atomic number but different mass numbers. They have similar chemical properties but different nuclear properties.
  5. A molecule may contain atoms of the same element (O₂) or different elements (H₂O). Ionic compounds such as NaCl are represented by formula units.
  6. Valency describes combining capacity and helps write chemical formulae. The total positive and negative charge in an ionic formula must balance.
  7. The mole is the SI unit for amount of substance. One mole contains approximately 6.022 × 10²³ specified entities (Avogadro constant).
  8. Relative atomic and molecular masses are compared with one-twelfth the mass of a carbon-12 atom.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • neutrons = mass number − atomic number
  • moles n = mass/molar mass
  • number of particles = n × 6.022 × 10²³

3. Worked examples

An atom has atomic number 11 and mass number 23. How many neutrons?

Answer: 12

Neutrons = 23 − 11 = 12.
