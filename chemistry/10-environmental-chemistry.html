Environmental Chemistry

RRB Group D General Science · Chemistry · Chapter 21

About this chapter

Environmental chemistry studies chemical processes in air, water and soil and how pollutants affect ecosystems and health.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Air pollution includes particulate matter, sulfur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide, ground-level ozone and volatile organic compounds.
  2. Sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides can form acids in the atmosphere, contributing to acid rain that damages aquatic ecosystems, vegetation and buildings.
  3. The enhanced greenhouse effect results from increased concentrations of greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide, methane and nitrous oxide.
  4. Ozone in the stratosphere absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation; ground-level ozone is a pollutant that irritates the respiratory system.
  5. Water pollution can arise from sewage, industrial effluents, fertilisers, pesticides, oil and plastics. Treatment and source control are essential.
  6. Eutrophication occurs when excess nutrients stimulate algal growth; decomposition can reduce dissolved oxygen and harm aquatic life.
  7. Biodegradable materials can be decomposed by microorganisms under suitable conditions; non-biodegradable waste can persist and accumulate.
  8. The 3Rs are reduce, reuse and recycle. Waste segregation, safe disposal, cleaner production and conservation protect environmental quality.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • greenhouse gases: CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, water vapour
  • acid rain precursors: SO₂ and NOₓ
