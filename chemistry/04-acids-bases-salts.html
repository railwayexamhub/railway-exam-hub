Acids, Bases and Salts

RRB Group D General Science · Chemistry · Chapter 15

About this chapter

Acids and bases are important classes of substances identified by their behaviour in water and with indicators.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. Arrhenius acids produce H⁺ (more accurately H₃O⁺) in aqueous solution; bases produce OH⁻ ions in aqueous solution.
  2. Acids turn blue litmus red; bases turn red litmus blue. Universal indicator gives a range of colours related to pH.
  3. The pH scale is commonly 0–14 at room temperature: below 7 acidic, 7 neutral and above 7 alkaline. pH is logarithmic.
  4. Neutralisation is reaction of an acid with a base to form salt and water; it is generally exothermic.
  5. Strong acids ionise extensively in water; weak acids ionise partially. Strength is not the same as concentration.
  6. Common acids: hydrochloric acid in gastric juice, sulfuric acid in batteries, acetic acid in vinegar and citric acid in citrus fruits.
  7. Common bases: sodium hydroxide, potassium hydroxide, calcium hydroxide and magnesium hydroxide. Alkalis are bases soluble in water.
  8. Salts are ionic compounds. Baking soda is sodium hydrogen carbonate (NaHCO₃); washing soda is sodium carbonate decahydrate (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O); bleaching powder is commonly represented as CaOCl₂.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]
  • acid + base → salt + water
  • HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

3. Worked examples

A solution has pH 3. Is it acidic or basic?

Answer: Acidic

A pH below 7 is acidic.
