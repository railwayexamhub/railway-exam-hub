Chemical Reactions and Equations

RRB Group D General Science · Chemistry · Chapter 14

About this chapter

A chemical reaction converts reactants into products by rearranging atoms and changing chemical bonds.

Study method: Understand each concept, learn the important relations and units, work through the examples, then attempt all 25 questions and review the explanations.

1. Complete concept notes

  1. A chemical equation represents a reaction using symbols and formulae. It must be balanced because atoms are conserved.
  2. Combination reaction: two or more reactants form one product. Decomposition: one compound breaks into simpler substances.
  3. Displacement reaction: a more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound. Double displacement involves exchange of ions.
  4. Oxidation can mean addition of oxygen or removal of hydrogen; reduction can mean removal of oxygen or addition of hydrogen. Modernly, oxidation is electron loss and reduction electron gain.
  5. Redox reactions involve oxidation and reduction together. Corrosion of iron is an oxidation process requiring oxygen and moisture.
  6. Exothermic reactions release heat; endothermic reactions absorb heat. Combustion is commonly exothermic.
  7. A precipitate is an insoluble solid formed when solutions react. Gas evolution, colour change and temperature change can indicate a reaction.
  8. Catalysts alter reaction rate without being consumed overall; they do not change the equilibrium position in a reversible reaction.

2. Important formulas and facts

  • mass of reactants = mass of products in a closed system
  • oxidation = loss of electrons
  • reduction = gain of electrons

3. Worked examples

Balance: H₂ + O₂ → H₂O.

Answer: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

There are 4 hydrogen and 2 oxygen atoms on both sides.
