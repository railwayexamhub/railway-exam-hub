Time, Speed and Distance

RRB Group D Mathematics • Chapter 12

About this chapter

Speed relates distance travelled to time taken. Keep units consistent before calculating.

Study tip: Read the rules, practise the worked examples, then attempt the quiz without looking at the notes.

1. Key concepts and rules

  • Convert minutes to hours when speed is in km/h.
  • For a fixed distance, higher speed means lower time.
  • Average speed is total distance divided by total time.
  • For equal distances at speeds a and b, average speed is 2ab/(a+b).

2. Important formulas and reminders

Speed = Distance ÷ Time
Distance = Speed × Time
Time = Distance ÷ Speed
1 m/s = 3.6 km/h
1 km/h = 5/18 m/s

3. Solved examples

Example 1: A vehicle travels 150 km in 3 hours. Find speed.

Speed = 150/3.

Answer: 50 km/h

Example 2: Convert 72 km/h to m/s.

72×5/18.

Answer: 20 m/s

Example 3: How far does a train travel at 60 km/h in 2.5 hours?

Distance = 60×2.5.

Answer: 150 km

4. Exam tips

  • Write down the given values before selecting a formula.
  • Check that units are consistent.
  • Do calculations step by step and simplify the final answer.
  • Estimate the result to catch arithmetic mistakes.
  • Practise questions under a time limit after learning the method.
